# Shared visual system

Approved 2026-10-28 after the independent UI review. This supersedes the earlier per-game serif, brass, county-fair, classified-file and emoji-led instruction treatments in `party-notebook.md` and the game-presentation paragraph in `spec.md`.

Keep Tracy / 10,000 days and the white Party notebook identity. Every game uses the same system sans-serif title, charcoal text, neutral surfaces, restrained outlines and shared spacing. Player emoji remain personal identifiers. Instruction lists use typography and dividers, not decorative emoji. Game objects use simple shapes with restrained inset depth; keep emoji out of reel symbols and asset marks. Colors identify outcomes, teams and red/blue betting. Central palette tokens own success, loss, betting colors and the quiet object outline; peach is the small celebration accent.

The landscape display has a consistent event header, title/timer frame, and a stage below. Introductions use spacious rule columns. Active games pair quieter instructions with the dominant machine, teams or results. The shell retains readable fallback layouts at narrower widths. All title treatments are shared; no per-game typeface overrides. Phone and host surfaces inherit the same palette and typography.

Use direct, personal copy: “Joining is optional”, “Bets are in”, “Investment results”, and “Keep your phone’s ringer on”. Remove invented casino slogans, spy-file metaphors and commentary that judges participation. Preserve game names, configured values, settlement rules and privacy boundaries.

Consolidate existing component rules when refining visuals. Avoid accumulating successive overrides for the same machine or display heading. Presentation motion remains driven by saved outcomes and retains reduced-motion behavior.

Festive balloons float up and fireworks burst behind every stage, on the TV at full size and on player pages smaller and with fewer bursts. Ambient fireworks use muted colors, fine rays and infrequent bursts so the content stays prominent. They never block content and sit behind it. (2026-10-28: the previous float animation used invalid CSS and never moved; it is fixed.) All celebration layers are non-interactive, pause in hidden tabs and are suppressed for reduced motion. Outcome fireworks (`src/client/celebrate.ts`) are event-driven: they fire once when a saved win or reveal arrives live, never on reload, and never for a net loss.

The TV is authored on a fixed 1920×1080 stage (`DisplayFrame` in `src/client/main.tsx`) that is scaled to fit any landscape screen wider than 1000px; `--vw` and `--vh` replace viewport units inside it so every round keeps the same composition. Narrow or portrait windows fall back to the fluid layout. Slots on the TV shows one anonymous dot per player in the room, filled as players spin (never who), and Trivia shows one card per team with the buzzing team and first player highlighted. Heroes idle gently (drifting machine, ringing phone) unless reduced motion is set.

The TV and phone answer different questions. The TV (landscape) shows shared state: a title band with the countdown and a progress bar, a slim one-row rules strip, a full-width stage, then a quiet room strip of player faces. It uses large type (no body text under about 18px) and, for Slots and Powerball, live public counts. The phone (portrait) shows private state: one identity row (avatar, name, Open/Closed pill, connection), the timer, the game, and a primary action that stays in thumb reach (Spin is pinned; Save bet is not, so it never covers the stake field). The phone has no ball machine at all because the TV is the stage; after a bet it says to watch the screen, and the result card that leads with the outcome appears when the TV animation ends. Both timers stay. Phase changes fade in on both surfaces.

Slots uses a ticket printer: a dark printer slot, perforated cream tickets with three monochrome SVG symbols that roll and stamp in, and compact tickets for earlier spins. Powerball uses a gumball machine: glass dome of red and blue balls, red base with a knob and door, and a capsule that rolls out and cracks open to the saved color. Both are drawn with CSS/SVG shapes only.

The costume display may show a friendly ghost before results, and the slots display may use a decorative slot-machine illustration alongside the existing rules. These are visual accents and do not introduce additional game copy or results.

Symbols and copy rules (2026-10-28 polish pass): ↗ marks only links that open a new tab; → marks moving forward; buttons that act in place carry no arrow. Every disclosure uses one ⌄ marker that rotates when open. Investment marks are plain three-letter tickers. Token amounts go through `formatTokens` (thousands separators, true minus). The warm-up round is called "Connection check" on every surface. Powerball uses "bet", never "ticket"; Slots uses "joining is optional". Disabled buttons use a faint fill with a dashed border.
